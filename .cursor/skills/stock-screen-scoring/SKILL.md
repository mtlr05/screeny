# Stock screen scoring

## Score (out of 7; 1 point each, partial credit as stated). Small/mid cap and US/Canada listing are screening filters, not scored.
1. Thesis with a secular runway or growth driver; cheap but no growth (asset value, cash pile, wind-down, low multiple only) = 0.5.
2. Return ~40%/yr from the publish-day close using the author's target and timeframe (default 2 years); 25-40%/yr = 0.5; below 25%/yr = 0.
3. CEO ownership or incentive, from the article or a cited filing only. Score the highest tier that applies. 0 when nothing is stated.
   - 1.0: the current CEO or founder-CEO owns at least 5%.
   - 0.75: the CEO or executive chair made an open-market purchase of shares.
   - 0.5: the CEO owns 1 to 5%, or holds a stated share count or dollar amount that the text does not show is under 1% of the company.
   - 0.5: a clear skin-in-the-game statement about the CEO or founder, even without a figure.
   - 0.5: a founder or chair who is not named as CEO owns at least 5%.
   - 0.25: aggregate insider, management, or director holding of at least 10%; a director or unnamed insider open-market purchase; or CEO or executive pay explicitly tied to performance, price hurdles, or TSR.
   - 0: buybacks alone; "founder-led" alone with no ownership or skin-in-the-game statement; ownership under 1%; a combined management-and-directors figure treated as the CEO's own stake (that figure is 0.25 aggregate at most, and only when it is at least 10%).
   `scripts/ceo_stake_grep.py` prints candidate sentences and a suggested tier. The scorer confirms the sentence. A suggested 0.5 for a bare share count becomes 0 when the same text shows the stake is under 1%.
4. Moat. Judge whether the business, as the article presents it, has one of the five types (intangibles, switching costs, network effects, cost advantage, efficient scale). Score Strong 1 / Moderate 0.75 / Narrow 0.5, and in the evidence sentence name the type, the mechanism, the trend, and the main threat. The author does not have to use the word moat, name the type, or spell out mechanism, trend, and threat. If those are obvious from the business model, the scorer supplies them. A bare label ("this company has a moat") with no business-model basis is still 0. Facts about this company must come from the article or a cited filing. The scorer is expected to know what the five types mean and may use established, non-controversial knowledge of how that kind of business competes — and must say so in the evidence sentence. Do not use a disputed or thesis-dependent claim the article does not support (for example, that the company is the low-cost producer, unless the article says so). None of the five types, or a type not among the five (for example correlation with bitcoin, a one-off contract, being cheap to book value, or a royalty/yield model with nothing else) = 0. An article is not dropped for scoring 0 here. If the moat is inferred and the article does not describe how durable it is, score Narrow 0.5 unless the text or that established knowledge supports a stronger, more durable advantage (patents or regulatory exclusivity with dates, limited competition, and the like can support Moderate 0.75).
5. Edge. Edge is about the people running or backing the company, shown by named, checkable evidence in the article or a cited filing: past results they delivered (a named prior company, deal or turnaround with figures), a record of meeting or beating guidance across several periods, capital allocation outcomes (buybacks at good prices, accretive acquisitions, returns on capital), or a founder who built the product and still runs it. A good guidance record supports it.
   - Strong 1: at least two independent kinds of evidence above, with figures, and directly relevant to this company's thesis (e.g. a repeat dealmaker with a named multi-bagger and a multi-year guidance record).
   - Moderate 0.75: one kind of evidence with figures and relevant to the thesis.
   - Narrow 0.5: one kind of evidence that is partial, dated, or only loosely relevant (e.g. long tenure or an award without results).
   - 0: assertion without checkable evidence (talented, experienced, visionary), insiders buying or ownership alone (that belongs to criterion 3), or a track record in an unrelated business.
   - Edge is not credit for a cheap valuation, a funding advantage, or a high yield; those are not edge.
6. Differential view. When a street revenue consensus exists, the score is the gap between the author's dollar revenue forecast and that consensus. That comparison is the whole test. The qualitative test at the end of this item is used only when no street number is available.
   - Street number: `python3 scripts/revenue_consensus.py TICKER YEAR`. The script prints Yahoo Finance `earningsTrend` `revenueEstimate.avg`, the analyst count, the period end, and the quoteSummary URL. That printed average is the consensus on the fetch date, not a reconstructed consensus as of the article's publish date. YEAR is the calendar year of that printed period end: a fiscal year that ends on 2027-01-31 is requested as 2027. Use the row whose end date is the fiscal year the article forecasts. Run it for each such year the article states as dollar revenue. When the article states no dollar revenue, run it for the current fiscal year and the next one. A row with no analysts is not a consensus. If Yahoo has no row for that year, a consensus revenue dollar figure the article itself states is the street number; quote that sentence.
   - A pair is comparable when the article states the author's revenue for a named fiscal year in dollars, the street number is for that same year, and both figures are in the same currency or the article states the FX rate used to convert them. A growth adjective or a CAGR with no dollar revenue is not a forecast for this test. When more than one year is comparable, use the year the article uses for the price target or the stated forecast horizon. If it does not single one out, use the nearest fiscal year and say so.
   - Gap = author / consensus - 1. Print that formula in the evidence sentence, with the year, both figures, the analyst count, and the source URL (the quoteSummary URL, or the article sentence when the street number comes from the article). At least 10% = 1. Above zero and under 10% = 0.5. Zero or negative = 0. Currencies that differ, with no rate in the article, = 0.5, and the evidence cites both figures. Do not switch to the qualitative test in that case.
   - Author revenue not stated, while Yahoo or the article has a consensus for at least one year, = 0. Cite the street figure, year, analyst count, and URL, and write "not stated". Do not use the qualitative test.
   - Qualitative test, only when the fetch fails, every annual row has zero analysts, or the author states a dollar revenue only for a year with no Yahoo row, and the article cites no consensus dollar figure for that year: the article names the specific reason the market is wrong (stigma or category label, a temporary or mundane issue, neglect) and why it should correct. Both named with evidence = 1; one missing or vague = 0.5; the pitch matches consensus = 0. An overreaction to temporary news supports a 1.
7. Dated catalyst within ~24 months that could force a re-rating = 1; named but undated = 0.5; none = 0.

## Evidence rules (so two runs score the same text the same way; write one sentence of evidence per criterion in the one-pager)
- CEO stake (3): judged from the article or a cited filing only. If neither states it, score 0 and print 'not stated'. Never use unstated outside knowledge, and never invent a rule such as 'silent = 0' beyond this. The evidence sentence quotes or cites the matched sentence.
- Return (2): compute from the stated target and horizon with `px.py` (publish-day close) and print the formula; no stated target = 0 unless the article gives a clear valuation endpoint.
  - A "clear valuation endpoint" includes a target computed from a multiple the author says the stock should reach, using the author's own earnings, cash flow or asset figure; label it "implied". A current multiple alone ("trades at 5.8x forward earnings") is not an endpoint.
  - Price is the close on the publish date, or the next trading day if that date is a weekend or holiday. Horizon is the author's stated horizon, else 2 years; say which. A target range uses its midpoint; a bull case alone is used and labelled "bull case". Return per year = (target / price)^(1 / years) - 1.
- Moat (4): the evidence sentence names the type, the mechanism, the trend, and the main threat. The author does not have to spell those out. If they are obvious from the business model, the scorer supplies them. Company facts come from the article or a cited filing. Established, non-controversial knowledge of how that kind of business competes may be used, and the sentence must say so. A bare label with no business-model basis is 0. Do not use a disputed or thesis-dependent claim the article does not support. If the moat is inferred and the article does not describe how durable it is, score Narrow 0.5 unless the text or that knowledge supports a stronger advantage.
- Edge (5): its own evidence sentence, from named checkable evidence in the article or a cited filing only. Outside knowledge does not count. Moat and edge are separate points, not a choice between the two.
- Differential view (6): when a street revenue consensus exists, score the gap, not the qualitative test. The Yahoo consensus is the figure on the fetch date, not on the publish date. Gap = author / consensus - 1. At least 10% above = 1; above zero and under 10% = 0.5; zero or negative = 0. No author dollar revenue while a consensus exists for a covered year = 0, and the evidence sentence says "not stated" and cites the street figure, year, analyst count, and URL. Currencies that differ, with no rate in the article, = 0.5, with both figures cited. The qualitative test (both the mispricing cause and why it corrects = 1; one missing or vague = 0.5; matches consensus = 0) runs only when the Yahoo fetch fails, every annual row has zero analysts, or the author's dollar revenue is only for a year with no Yahoo row, and the article cites no consensus dollar figure. The evidence sentence prints the formula, the year, both figures, the analyst count, and the source URL.
- Dated catalyst (7): 1 only for a named dated event within 24 months; 0.5 for a named undated one.
  - A scheduled earnings report counts as dated only if the article ties it to the thesis; a routine report date otherwise gets 0.5.
- Store `sc` as seven numbers, in criterion order, and `score_out_of`: 7. A stored `sc` of six numbers is the old rubric: the fourth number is moat (criterion 4), edge is 0, the fifth number is criterion 6, the sixth number is criterion 7, and `score_out_of` is 6. Do not treat that total as out of 7.
- Persist criterion scores and the evidence sentence per criterion; the report is regenerated from the stored scores so the same scores always give the same report.

## Worked examples

These five pitches show the rules above. Criterion 6 in each one is the qualitative test, the fallback used only when no street revenue consensus is available. They were not rescored against street revenue. Figures are the article's own targets and the publish-day closes used when the return was computed. The article's earlier close is not the price. Return per year = (target / price)^(1 / years) - 1. Nothing here is a quote of the write-up. The note after them illustrates criterion 4 when the scorer infers an obvious moat. It is not a sixth scored pitch.

### PRPO

Published 2026-10-12. The stated endpoint is a 2031 price of $160, the probability-weighted case, not the bull case and not the $45–$52 figure. That $45–$52 range is $160 discounted back to today, so using it as the target counts the discount twice and lands near 16%/yr (score 0). Horizon is the author's 2031 date, about 5.22 years from the publish date, not the 2-year default. Price is the publish-day close ($22.59 in the scored pass; the article's $23.50 is the prior session). (160 / 22.59)^(1 / 5.22) - 1 ≈ 46%/yr, so criterion 2 is 1. Label the target stated, not implied.

Thesis is 1: on-site cancer testing with a growth path, not a no-growth asset. Criterion 3 is 0.25, not "not stated". The article states the CEO owns about 2,850 shares. Against the stated share count that holding is under 1%, so the CEO's own stake is 0 rather than 0.5 for a bare share count. Officers and directors hold 16.6%. That is an aggregate figure of at least 10%, so it is 0.25, and it is not the CEO's stake. The evidence sentence cites those two statements. Highest tier is 0.25. Moat (4) is narrow, 0.5: a razor-and-blade kit with switching costs from the instrument and training; the threat is slow conversion of new labs. Edge (5) is narrow, 0.5: the founder built the product and still runs it, with no separate result that carries figures. Both count. Differential view (6) is 1: the shares still carry the revenue multiple of the cash-burn years, and the correction is proof of new-lab sales plus cash flow. Catalyst (7) is 1: November 2026 results are tied to pipeline conversion and cash flow, and the March 2027 annual report is tied to whether the substantial-doubt language comes off. Those are thesis-linked dates, not a routine earnings stamp. Total 5.25/7.

### MGM

Published 2026-10-13. Fair value is the base sum of the parts, about $45. There is no horizon, so say the default of 2 years. Do not use the failed $48.30 bid; the text gives that bid no value. Price is the publish-day close ($29.40 in the scored pass; the article's $29.27 is three sessions earlier). (45 / 29.40)^(1 / 2) - 1 ≈ 24%/yr, which is below 25%, so criterion 2 is 0. The same formula on $29.27 is still about 24%.

Thesis is 0.5: a cheap lease-adjusted cash flow and buybacks, with softer room rates, not a secular growth driver. CEO stake is 0 and "not stated"; a 26.5% outside holder is not the CEO. Moat (4) is moderate, 0.75: the Strip portfolio is efficient scale and an intangible, the mechanism is the resorts under the lease, the trend is weaker room revenue, and the threat is rent that does not fall. Edge (5) is moderate, 0.75: about $9.7 billion of buybacks cut the share count by about half, at an average price under $40. Both count. Differential view (6) is 1: the market is treating a financing failure as a broken business, and the correction is that rates, not the resorts, ended the bid. Catalyst (7) is 1: the October 28 quarterly report is tied to the thesis (an easy Las Vegas comparison and whether buybacks resumed). A routine report date with no such tie would be 0.5. Total 4/7.

### BLSH

Published 2026-10-13. The text gives a range of $40–$44 if the multiple re-rates, and a separate tokenization case near $50. A range uses its midpoint, so the target is $42, labelled implied (a multiple the text says the shares should reach). The $50 case is not used once a range exists; it would be the input only if it were the only case, and then it would be labelled "bull case". A current forward multiple is not an endpoint. No horizon is stated, so say 2 years. The article does not print a share price; the scored pass used the publish-day close of $31.77. (42 / 31.77)^(1 / 2) - 1 ≈ 15%/yr, so criterion 2 is 0. The unused $50 case would be about 25%/yr and would still not replace the midpoint.

Thesis is 1: tokenized assets and a shift toward subscription revenue are a growth driver. CEO stake is 0 and "not stated". Moat (4) is 0: correlation with bitcoin and a transfer-agent deal are not one of the five moat types. Edge (5) is 0: no named operating result, guidance record, or founder who built the product. Differential view (6) is 0.5: a discount to a historical multiple is named, but not a specific mispricing cause and a reason it corrects. Catalyst (7) is 0.5: tokenization and the transfer-agent deal are named and undated. None would be 0; undated is 0.5. Total 2/7.

### STNE

Published 2026-10-09. There is no price target and no valuation endpoint. A forward multiple of 5.8x is the current multiple, which the evidence rule says is not an endpoint, so criterion 2 is 0. Do not invent a target multiple the text never applies to an earnings figure.

Thesis is 1: the relaunched credit book is a growth driver, not a low-multiple-only story. CEO stake is 0 and "not stated". Moat (4) is 0: a tighter credit process is not one of the five moat types. Edge (5) is 0: no checkable record of the people running it. Differential view (6) is 1: the market is still pricing a repeat of the earlier credit failure, and the correction is more evidence that provisions and the receivables check are holding. Catalyst (7) is 1: the October 25 runoff is a named date inside 24 months and the text ties it to a possible re-rating. It is not a routine earnings date. Total 3/7.

### ONDS

Published 2026-10-12. The text builds bear, middle, and bull prices and says the bull case is the one it adopts: $20.87 at the end of 2027, from the author's revenue and a sales multiple. Label it "bull case". Do not average it with the middle case. Horizon is end-2027, about 1.22 years (14.6 months), not 2 years. Price is the publish-day close ($6.67 in the scored pass; the article's $6.73 is the prior session). (20.87 / 6.67)^(1 / 1.22) - 1 ≈ 155%/yr, so criterion 2 is 1. The middle case would also clear 40%/yr, but it is not the adopted target.

Thesis is 1: a defense-prime buildout with a stated revenue ramp, not a cash-pile story. CEO stake is 0 and "not stated"; insider selling without a share count is not a stake. Moat (4) is moderate, 0.75: prime-contractor status is efficient scale (the firm owns the customer vehicle), the mechanism and the budget trend are described, and the threat is dilution plus execution. Edge (5) is 0: insider selling is not an operating record. Differential view (6) is 1: the market is pricing the share-count explosion rather than the cash and backlog, and the correction is revenue catching that share count. Catalyst (7) is 1: platform EBITDA profitability in Q4 2026 is a named quarter inside 24 months and is the thesis. A later share issuance is dilution, not the re-rating event. Total 4.75/7.

### Inferred moat

This note applies the general moat rule. It is not a special case, and it is not a scored pitch.

A pre-approval therapy that would be the first disease-modifying treatment in an indication with no approved disease-modifying therapy scores Narrow 0.5 as an intangible. The evidence sentence names the type (intangible), the mechanism (first disease-modifying therapy in that indication), the trend (still pre-approval), and the threat (approval risk or later entrants). Company facts come from the article. The sentence cites scorer knowledge for the fact that no disease-modifying therapy is approved. The score stays Narrow 0.5 because the article does not describe how durable the advantage is. Patents or regulatory exclusivity with dates, and limited competition, can support Moderate 0.75 under the same rule. A bare label ("this company has a moat") with no business-model basis is still 0.
